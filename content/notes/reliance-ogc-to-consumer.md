Reliance is several businesses glued together by capital allocation. Reading any one segment in isolation is how I used to get the quarter wrong.

## Oil to chemicals

Crack spreads and GRMs still dominate the print. That is not a mystery. The question I write down each quarter is narrower:

- Are downstream spreads paying for the integrated O2C complex, or is the complex only working when cracks are generous?
- How much of the “chemicals” commentary is volume versus realisation?

I do not need a precise GRM forecast. I need to know whether the energy engine is covering the group’s investment pace or merely decorating it.

## Retail and consumer

Store adds are easy to count. Throughput per store and mix (grocery versus fashion versus electronics) are slower. I care more about whether retail is becoming a cash business that can fund itself, or a scale story that still leans on the parent.

## How I hold the whole

Three ledgers, one balance sheet:

1. Energy cash generation
2. Consumer and digital reinvestment
3. Net debt and project pipeline

If (1) is weak while (3) is busy, the story is about patience and funding, not about the next store opening. If (1) is solid, I can read retail and digital as compounding rather than as a claim on the parent.

This is a map, not a target price. The map is useful when a headline from one segment tries to stand in for the group.
