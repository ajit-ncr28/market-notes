IT services commentary often stops at “deal wins.” That is a bookings number. The conversion into revenue and then into margin is a different chain.

## The series I keep

| Lens | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| TCV / large-deal TCV | Demand signal, not earnings |
| Book-to-bill | Whether bookings are running ahead of revenue |
| Utilisation (ex-trainees) | Near-term margin lever |
| Attrition | Wage and pyramid pressure |
| Subcon share | Flexibility versus cost |

## A working interpretation

A strong TCV quarter with soft utilisation usually means the book is building ahead of staffing. That can be fine. It becomes a problem only if the pyramid cannot absorb the work without subcontractors or lateral hiring at the wrong levels.

I am less interested in the year-on-year constant-currency print in isolation. I want to know:

- Is discretionary work still thin, with cost-takeout and vendor consolidation carrying the book?
- Are fixed-price programmes a larger share, which changes how utilisation shows up?
- Has the onsite mix moved enough to matter for margins?

## What would change my note

If large-deal TCV stays healthy for a few more quarters **and** utilisation stops being the residual that has to do all the work, the cycle argument gets simpler. Until then I treat TCV as a leading indicator with a wide error band, not as a substitute for the margin bridge.
