This is not a reconstruction of a clever decision. It is a record of how I behaved when a bank holding I understood reasonably well went quiet, then cheaper, then cheaper again.

## What I thought I knew

I had a working picture of the book: slippages were not exploding, the deposit franchise was not falling apart, and the capital position did not look stretched. That picture did not change much in the first few months of the drawdown.

What changed was the multiple other people were willing to pay for that picture.

## Where I wobbled

I opened the chart more often than the filings. That is usually the tell. I started writing “maybe the market sees credit costs I do not” without actually listing what those costs would be.

The useful question was not “is this cheap.” It was:

- Has my original thesis broken, in writing?
- Am I adding because the facts improved, or because the price fell?
- Would I still want this book if I could not look at the quote for a year?

## What I kept

I wrote a one-page note every month: asset quality, deposit mix, and one sentence on why I still owned it. Some months the sentence was thin. Those months were information.

I did not need a heroic ending. I needed a rule I could reuse: **when the multiple moves and the book does not, slow down instead of narrating.**

The ticker in the frontmatter is just the specimen. The note is about the habit.
