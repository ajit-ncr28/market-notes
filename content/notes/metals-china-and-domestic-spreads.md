Nifty Metal is a convenient bucket. It is also a lazy one. A China stimulus headline and a domestic spread squeeze can live in the same week and mean opposite things for cash flows.

## Two clocks

**Clock A — China and seaborne prices.** Iron ore, coking coal, and the tone of property-and-infrastructure demand. This is the weather system everyone watches.

**Clock B — Indian spreads.** HRC / TMT realisations versus raw material baskets, inventory days at mills and trade, and the mix of exports versus domestic.

Clock A moves the index. Clock B moves the companies I actually read.

## A simple checklist

- Has the domestic spread widened or narrowed over the last 6–8 weeks, not just the last session?
- Are mills cutting or raising offers into the next month?
- Is coking coal doing more damage than realisations can offset?
- Are export realisations a relief valve or a drag?

## Sector view, held loosely

I treat a metals rally built only on Clock A as a sentiment note. I treat a quarter where Clock B improves — even if the index is quiet — as the more durable observation.

No need to dress that up as a call on the index. It is just a way to stop mixing the weather with the harvest.
